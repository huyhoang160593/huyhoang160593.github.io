# Bee and PuppyCat — research notes for a site design brief

Research purpose: extract the show's _visual and tonal DNA_ from primary sources, then turn it
into design tokens for this site (Astro 7 + Tailwind 4 + daisyUI 5, vi/en bilingual).

Sourcing rule used throughout: a claim carries a bare URL next to it. First-party material
(Frederator's own site, the creator's and art director's own pages, Netflix's own title page,
the composer's own release metadata, an artist's own upload) is treated as primary. Where only a
secondary source could be found, that is stated in the sentence. Anything I decided myself is
tagged `[inference]`. Nothing here is presented as sourced unless it is.

## 2. Tone, genre, audience

**Premise: magical-girl structure wearing a temp-job premise.** Netflix's own page tags the show
"Adult Animation, US, Sci-Fi Shows, **Teen TV Shows**, TV Comedies":
<https://www.netflix.com/title/81245457>. Frederator's own logline frames Bee as a "reluctant
hero" in "Fishbowl Space": <https://frederatorstudios.com/shows/bee-puppycat/>.

**Genre blend.** Slice-of-life + science fiction + workplace comedy. Allegri, on her own taste:
she "loves slice-of-life" and "my favorite anime is _Sailor Moon_, where everyone is really
pretty":
<https://comicsalliance.com/natasha-allegri-interview-bee-puppycat>.
The workplace half is literal in the art: she notes the **turtle posters at the temp agency** as a
production detail, and that Bee's own apartment was rebuilt so the set could tell her story:
<https://www.vulture.com/2014/11/top-female-filmmaker-kickstarter-natasha-allegri-bee-puppycat.html>.

**Emotional register — slow, quiet, melancholy, low-stakes.** Allegri names Miyazaki's _Kiki's
Delivery Service_ as the influence she returns to, watching it monthly for seven years, and talks
about pacing and quiet rather than plot escalation:
<https://www.vulture.com/2014/11/top-female-filmmaker-kickstarter-natasha-allegri-bee-puppycat.html>.
Dialogue was modelled on **naturalistic, overlapping anime speech** rather than cartoon
punchline timing: same URL.

**Audience and rating.** Netflix's own page in the locale I read shows the maturity line **"T13"**,
alongside the genres above (<https://www.netflix.com/title/81245457>). Ratings strings on Netflix
are locale- and region-dependent, so **treat "T13" as a locale label, not a global rating**; the
stable, primary facts are the genre/tag list and the "Teen TV Shows"/"Adult Animation" pairing on
that page.

**Merch and brand reach** (useful for knowing how the brand is presented officially): an official
store selling apparel, plush and stationery under the _Bee and PuppyCat_ wordmark:
<https://beeandpuppycat.shop/>. Frederator also ran a production/prop merch line and talked about
limited-edition postcards: <https://frederatorstudios.com/look-first-of-all-bee-and-puppycat-is-a-fantastic-show-yes-second-natasha/>.

## 3. Art style and visual language

### Character design

- **Bee is deliberately round, not slim.** Allegri: Bee "got chubbier as time went on… I think
  chubby things are really cute":
  <https://www.vulture.com/2014/11/top-female-filmmaker-kickstarter-natasha-allegri-bee-puppycat.html>.
- **PuppyCat's markings are drawn from memory and are deliberately wrong.** Allegri describes
  drawing Shiba Inu markings from memory and "backward", and notes his heavy brow reads as
  permanently disapproving even when he is not angry: same Vulture URL.
- **Voice as design:** PuppyCat's voice comes from the Vocaloid voice bank **OLIVER** — a
  synthetic, flat, slightly uncanny register that matches his deadpan brow: same Vulture URL.
- Designers on the series are named as **Becky Dreistadt** and **Hans Tseng** in the
  ComicsAlliance interview (link above), and Tseng's own site credits him with "Main
  character designs": <http://hanstseng.art/bee-and-puppycat/>.

### Backgrounds, colour and line

- **Story before scenery.** Allegri describes the backgrounds becoming more important over the run:
  Bee's apartment was rebuilt so the set itself would tell her story, and Deckard's plants are
  specifically **a chef's herbs** — props with a character reason
  (<https://comicsalliance.com/natasha-allegri-interview-bee-puppycat>).
- **The "colour line" treatment.** Allegri credits art director **Efrain Farias** with introducing
  coloured contour lines in place of black outlines, and says "the thick line was something we
  tried": same ComicsAlliance URL.
- **Colour and lighting carry the mood.** Allegri: "atmosphere… Ephraim knows a lot about how
  color and lighting can change the mood": same ComicsAlliance URL. The two first-party sources
  spell this name differently — the interview prints "Ephraim Farias", while the pilot's own
  credit list and Frederator's own post both print "Efrain Farias" — so match whichever source you
  quote rather than picking a favourite.
- **First background = style bible.** Tseng's own caption for "Bee's Apartment BG Design and Paint"
  reads "**first BG created used to set the style and tone of overall series**":
  <http://hanstseng.art/bee-and-puppycat/>. The same portfolio lists "Color Script" and
  "Composite Notes" among the artefacts he kept: same URL.
- Frederator's own post is blunt about where the effort went — "backgrounds are the best
  backgrounds" — with a room-by-room background gallery:
  <https://frederatorstudios.com/beeandpuppycatbee-and-puppycat-lazy-in-space-backgrounds-are-the-best-backgrounds-now-whose-bedroom-could/>.
- **Gouache/paper-and-paint finish.** The backgrounds read as soft, opaque, painterly media with
  visible brush softening at edges. `[inference]` — I found no first-party statement naming the
  physical medium, so treat this as a look I am describing, not a documented fact.

### Influences, traced to the creators' own statements (not to a third party)

- **Sailor Moon** — favourite anime; the source of the show's pretty-cast, sparkle-heavy
  vocabulary:
  <https://comicsalliance.com/natasha-allegri-interview-bee-puppycat>.
- **Studio Ghibli / Miyazaki**, specifically _Kiki's Delivery Service_ — watched monthly for seven
  years: <https://www.vulture.com/2014/11/top-female-filmmaker-kickstarter-natasha-allegri-bee-puppycat.html>.
- **VHS-era anime and manga generally**, plus _Ranma ½_, _Oh My Goddess_ and _Garfield_, with manga
  from her Japanese grandmother as the entry point:
  <https://comicsalliance.com/natasha-allegri-interview-bee-puppycat>.

### Ornament: sparkles and bows

Allegri, in her own words: "**sparkles — I just really like it, I put it everywhere**" and
"**I put bows on everything**":
<https://comicsalliance.com/natasha-allegri-interview-bee-puppycat>.
This is the single most usable design instruction in the whole research set: ornament is not
decoration bolted on afterwards, it is the show's stated aesthetic position.

- **Logo:** "I designed the new logo" — Hans Tseng, art director / BG design / character design:

  <https://web.archive.org/web/20200827034628/https://www.behance.net/gallery/9837545/Bee-and-PuppyCat>.

- Score by **Will Wiesenfeld** (pilot credit list, Behance Wayback link above); released officially

  <https://www.deezer.com/us/album/449683475>, <https://www.deezer.com/us/album/449683605>.
- Track names carry the show's own vocabulary and read like set-pieces of a place rather than

  "Warlocks", "Planet Clown", "Planet Mermaid Resort", "Galactic Bloom", "End Credits":
  same album URLs. This is a _lullaby-and-toy-instrument_ register more than an action register.
  `[inference]` on the "toy instrument" characterisation; the track titles are hard sources.
- **Natasha Allegri sings on at least one cue** — "Space Lullaby (feat. Natasha Allegri)":
  <https://www.deezer.com/us/album/449683475>.
- PuppyCat's vocal texture is a Vocaloid (**OLIVER**), i.e. synthetic-by-design:
  <https://www.vulture.com/2014/11/top-female-filmmaker-kickstarter-natasha-allegri-bee-puppycat.html>.

### Motion quirks

Honest state: **no primary statement on motion design or timing was found.** The pilot's own credits
name the director, **Larry Leichliter**
(<https://web.archive.org/web/20200827034628/https://www.behance.net/gallery/9837545/Bee-and-PuppyCat>),
and the animation services studios are listed in section 1. Anything more specific below is
`[inference]` from the show itself and from how the interviews describe pacing:

- Long holds on static painted backgrounds, then a short burst of limited animation — the "quiet
  then pop" rhythm Allegri attributes to her Miyazaki habit (Vulture URL above). `[inference]`
- Transformation/wardrobe beats and chibi cutaways used as anime-vocabulary punctuation.
  `[inference]`
- Holds broken by small loops (blinking, floating hair, slow drifts) rather than full-body
  movement. `[inference]`

## 4. "Visual DNA" — the seven traits worth stealing

Derived strictly from the sourced traits in section 3; the _design_ translation is mine.

| #   | Trait                                                    | Backed by                                                                                        | Website translation `[inference]`                                                       |
| --- | -------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | **Coloured outline, not black outline**                  | Farias' colour-line pass, Allegri's own account (ComicsAlliance)                                 | borders and rules use a warm tinted ink, never `#000`; focus rings in the accent colour |
| 2   | **Round, soft, low-detail shapes**                       | Bee's chubby-ification; Tseng's rounded logo                                                     | large corner radii, pill chips, no hard 90° panels                                      |
| 3   | **Sparkle and bow ornament as a rule, not an exception** | "I put it everywhere", "I put bows on everything"                                                | a reusable star/sparkle glyph sprinkled at low frequency; decorative separators         |
| 4   | **Backgrounds that tell a story**                        | Bee's rebuilt apartment; Deckard's chef's-herb plants; "first BG used to set the style and tone" | illustrated/patterned page backgrounds with intent; content-tinted surfaces per section |

| 6   | **Per-episode title cards treated as posters**           | Frederator's title-card statement                                                                | every page opens with a "title card" header block, not a generic hero                   |

## 5. Design tokens for this site

Context for the tokens: this repo runs Astro 7 with Tailwind 4 and daisyUI 5
(`package.json`), and `src/styles/global.css` currently registers two daisyUI themes —
`retro` (default) and `synthwave`. The tokens below replace that pair with two measured
palettes. Each colour is still selected through the traits in section 4, so it carries a
reason; what separates this from an inferred palette is that **every hex is a measured
value from a first-party background painting**, and the trait column names the trait it
answers instead of guessing at one.

No show artwork is redistributed. Images are cited by URL only and never committed to this
repo, and no colour is lifted from a logo, character or title card — only the average
colours of painted backgrounds.

### 5.1 How the palettes were measured

The method, stated once for both palettes:

- decode the source image and sample its pixels on a grid (about 30 000 samples regardless
  of image size);
- run **k-means with `k = 12`** initialised from a fixed seed, drop clusters below 0.4 %
  weight, merge clusters whose centroids sit closer than a **merge distance of 8** in RGB,
  and sort the survivors dark → light.

The two settings are load-bearing. At a coarser merge (for instance 14) the small, saturated
clusters are absorbed into their neighbours and the accents disappear: the cat café loses
its red roof and its near-white clouds, which are exactly the colours the palette has to
keep. `k = 12` gives those accents a cluster of their own; a merge distance of 8 folds only
near-identical clusters. The scripts are reproducible outside this repo and were re-run to
confirm every value below.

### 5.2 Light theme — `cat-cafe`

Measured from the **summer panel** (top-left) alone of
<https://hanstseng.art/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/cat-cafe-2-summer-scaled.jpg>; the scene
is a cat café. The file is one scene drawn four times, once per season, in a 2×2 sheet, and
only the top-left summer panel is measured. The other three panels are not averaged in,
because four lighting conditions averaged together would mute the summer accents the
palette exists to carry.

| Hex       | Role in the theme              | daisyUI slot              | Trait                      |
| --------- | ------------------------------ | ------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| `#f3fdfb` | page ground                    | `base-100`                | 4 — painted ground         |
| `#f8e2d5` | raised surface, quiet panel    | `base-200`                | 2 — soft shapes            |
| `#e5da8e` | warning / highlight            | `warning`                 | 3 — ornament voice         |
| `#9ce8ef` | info / cool fill               | `info`                    | 5 — mood carried by colour |
| `#adbfab` | hairline, quiet success        | `base-300`, `success`     | 1 — tinted line            |
| `#3d88a0` | primary, links                 | `primary`                 | 5 — mood carried by colour |

### 5.4 Type

Constraint that matters on this repo: the site is **bilingual vi/en** with Vietnamese as the
default locale (`astro.config.mjs`), so any face chosen must ship a **Vietnamese** subset.
Verified against Google Fonts' own font metadata
(<https://fonts.google.com/metadata/fonts> — read 2026-10-17):

| Family          | Category  | Has `vietnamese` subset                                            |
| --------------- | --------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Baloo 2**     | Display   | ✅                                                                 |
| **Nunito**      | Sans      | ✅                                                                 |
| **Nunito Sans** | Sans      | ✅                                                                 |
| **Quicksand**   | Sans      | ✅ (rounder, lighter than Nunito)                                  |
| **Space Mono**  | Monospace | ✅                                                                 |
| **Fredoka**     | Sans      | ❌ — **no Vietnamese subset; do not use as the display face here** |

The decision:

- **Display — `Baloo 2`** (SIL OFL, Vietnamese ✅). Rounded, slightly retro, heavy weights
  hold up at poster size; matches trait 2 and the "title card as poster" habit (trait 6).
- **Body — `Nunito Sans`** (SIL OFL, Vietnamese ✅). Open counters, comfortable at 16–18px,
  keeps long Vietnamese diacritic runs legible.
- **Deferred — `Space Mono`** (SIL OFL, Vietnamese ✅). Deliberately not shipped: no
  component needs monospace today, so a third family would be unused weight. It is the face
  to reach for when one does.

Both shipped families are self-hosted with the Vietnamese and latin subsets only and
`font-display: swap`; the site adds no third-party font request.

### 5.5 Contour, grain and depth

From trait 1 (coloured line) and trait 2 (soft shapes):

- **Border: `2px`.** Thick enough to read as a drawn contour rather than a UI hairline, and
  drawn in the theme's ink — `base-content` (`#373853` on `cat-cafe`, `#d4fbf9` on
  `game-center`) — never `#000`. This is the daisyUI `--border` value.
- **Noise: `1` on `cat-cafe`, `0` on `game-center`.** Paper grain belongs on the near-white
  ground; on the saturated indigo ground it reads as dirt, so the dark theme turns it off.
- **Depth: `0` on both.** daisyUI's `--depth` adds a soft 3D shadow to components; the
  contour language instead draws its own hard, unblurred offset shadow (a sticker offset,
  trait 2), and the two must not both be on. Pressing an element translates it into its own
  shadow rather than softening it.
- **Radii** follow trait 2 — round, pill selectors — but the exact values are settled with
  the component treatments, not here.

**Deliberately out of scope for this pass: the ornament budget.** Trait 3 is real — "I put
it everywhere" — but a star field, a sparkle glyph and a scanline are each cheap to add and
easy to over-add, so the frequency budget that keeps them charming is a later decision. This
brief records the intent; it does not ship ornament.

### 5.6 Component treatments, in intent

Written against the components that already exist in this repo —
`src/components/PostCard.astro` (which uses `card card-border bg-base-100`) and
`src/layouts/BaseLayout.astro`. These map the show's ideas onto that surface; the names
below are daisyUI slots and the measured palette above, not new tokens.

**a. Post card** — the show's "painted room with a title on it". A `base-200` panel carrying
a `2px` `base-content` contour and a hard offset shadow in the ink colour; on hover it lifts
and its shadow grows, on the overshoot curve. Title in `Baloo 2`; date line small and
letterspaced; the single hot accent (`error` on `cat-cafe`, `accent` on `game-center`)
appears as one sparkle on hover only.

**b. Nav bar** — thin and quiet. A `base-100` strip with a `2px` `base-content` rule along
the bottom and a translucent blur behind it. Wordmark in `Baloo 2`, set as text: **do not
reproduce the show's logo art.** The active link gets a wavy 2px underline — a hand-drawn
feel from a CSS primitive.

**c. Tag chip** — the sparkle/bow unit. A pill with a `2px` ink contour and a low tint of
the tag's own colour as its fill, so a tag list reads like a colour-coded sticker sheet;
pressing flattens the shadow into the contour.

**d. Page header / "title card"** — the strongest idea in the show to reuse. A full-width
`base-200` panel framed by `2px` ink rules above and below: an eyebrow in small letterspaced
caps (a date or a section name, like an episode slate), a `Baloo 2` title, and a description
in `Nunito Sans`. The same block is reused on the home page, the blog index, a post and the
404 — that repetition is what makes it read as a title card rather than a hero.

**e. Primary button** — a `primary`-filled control with a `base-content` contour and the
same sticker shadow; pressing translates it into its own shadow. Reserve the single hot
accent for exactly one thing per screen, so the loudest measured colour keeps meaning
something.
